# History and Introduction to Cloud Computing

## INTRODUCTION
In the early 2000s during the internet boom, Amazon.com faced severe internal operational bottlenecks as teams repeatedly built servers, storage, and databases from scratch for every new feature. 
To overcome these challenges, Amazon's leadership restructured its monolithic architecture into small, scalable, API-accessible, self-service internal services.

After resolving its internal infrastructure hurdles, Amazon realized it maintained idle data centers across the United States and Europe that incurred high maintenance and tax expenses. 
To offset these costs, Amazon began renting out its excess server capacity to startups, officially launching Amazon Web Services (AWS) in 2006, which helped early adopters like Airbnb and Netflix scale rapidly. Following AWS's success, other major tech providers entered the cloud space:

Microsoft Azure: Announced in 2008 and officially launched in 2010, leveraging Microsoft's enterprise software ecosystem.   
Google Cloud Platform (GCP): Originated as Google App Engine in 2008 and expanded into a comprehensive cloud platform in 2011.

In everyday terms, cloud computing powers common digital activities, from uploading photos and videos to Google Drive to streaming live sports and movies on platforms like Netflix.
